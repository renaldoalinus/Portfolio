## Overview

This project involved designing and shipping a B2C fitness platform for a high-profile fitness creator based in Bucharest. The goal was to transform an existing audience into a scalable digital product that allows users to discover, purchase, and follow personalized workout and nutrition plans.

The platform was designed and delivered end-to-end under a very tight timeline, with a strong focus on conversion, usability, and visual quality across desktop and mobile.


## My Role

Product Designer (UX, UI, Research)

End-to-end product design, research, stakeholder collaboration, and delivery under tight constraints.


## Problem

The client wanted to move beyond social media–driven engagement and create a reliable, self-serve product that could:

- Convert visitors into paying users

- Recommend relevant fitness and diet plans based on user needs

- Support subscriptions and one-time purchases

- Scale content over time without increasing complexity

Key constraints shaped the problem space:

- A single, non-technical stakeholder

- Fixed launch deadlines

- Limited opportunity for extensive discovery or experimentation

The challenge was to design a product that was clear, trustworthy, and easy to use, while remaining flexible enough to evolve post-launch.

## Scope & Trade-offs

Given the timeline and budget constraints, the first version focused on core functionality:

- Desktop and mobile support

- Account creation and authentication

- Payments, subscriptions, and plan access

- Before-and-after content and video library

We consciously de-scoped a planned calorie calculator feature. The concept was not sufficiently validated, and including it would have introduced unnecessary complexity without clear evidence of value.

These decisions allowed us to ship a coherent MVP on time.


## Research & Discovery

Research was pragmatic and lightweight, aligned with the project constraints.

I conducted:

- User interviews with women already training with the coach

- Early usability testing on key flows (quiz, plan selection, content access)

- Competitive analysis of fitness and subscription-based platforms

Insights informed:

- The structure and tone of the onboarding quiz

- The level of guidance required post-purchase

- How to balance motivation with clarity, particularly on mobile


## Collaboration & Delivery

I worked directly with:

- The client as the sole stakeholder

- A developer I sourced through my own network, responsible for implementation

My responsibilities included:

- UX and UI design

- User research and usability testing

- Stakeholder alignment and prioritization

- Defining scope and managing trade-offs

- Supporting implementation during development


## Post-Launch Iterations

After launch, we made targeted improvements based on real usage and feedback:

- A dedicated mobile-only hero was designed to improve navigation and visual balance

The workout video section was restructured as content grew, separating:

- Completed workouts

- Current workouts to be done

These changes improved usability and long-term maintainability without requiring a full redesign.

It might be that the site has had changes since my initial involvement, for which I am not responsible for.


## Key Learnings

- Strong prioritization is essential under tight timelines

- Shipping an MVP and iterating post-launch is often more effective than aiming for completeness

- Clear communication is critical when working with non-design stakeholders

- Visual quality plays a significant role in trust for B2C products
