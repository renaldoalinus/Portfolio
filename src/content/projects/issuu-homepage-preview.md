## Overview

At Issuu, I work as a Senior Product Designer in the Growth team. One of the key problems we identified was a drop-off in new users immediately after uploading their first PDF.

Users were excited to upload a document — but then hit a sign-up and payment wall before clearly understanding what Issuu could actually do for them.

Through continuous interviews with new users, we learned something simple but critical:

"People didn’t understand the product’s value before being asked to commit."

This project focused on solving that.


## The Problem

The original flow looked like this:

Upload PDF → Sign up → Payment wall → Product

Even though a free plan existed, users:

- landed directly inside the tool

- didn’t understand “content types”

- didn’t know what transformation had actually happened

- couldn’t clearly connect Issuu to their own use case

The result:

- confusion

- hesitation

- lower-paid conversion


## Research Insight

From interviews with first-time users, we heard patterns like:

“I don’t know what I can actually do here.”

“What is a flipbook exactly?”

“Why should I pay for this?”

The core issue wasn’t pricing.

It was unclear value before commitment.

This reframed the challenge:

"How might we show value before asking for signup or payment?"


## The Idea

Instead of gating the experience immediately after upload, we introduced a live product demo moment.

After uploading their PDF, users would see their document automatically transformed into different content types:

- Flipbook

- Article

- Social media post

- GIF

We call these Content Types.

Instead of explaining them with text, we let users interact with them directly.

## Why This Works

The solution addressed three friction points:

1. Reduced Cognitive Load

Users didn’t have to imagine what “flipbook” meant.

2. Ownership Effect

Seeing their own PDF transformed increased perceived value.

3. Delayed Commitment

Signup felt like a natural next step, not a barrier.


## Experimentation & Validation

We launched this as an A/B test.

- Control: Original upload → signup flow

- Variant: Upload → interactive demo → signup

After 4 weeks:

📈 +14% increase in paid conversion

In a high-volume B2C product, this had a significant revenue impact.


![](/work/issuu-homepage-preview/10.png)


## Phase 2: Optimizing the Homepage Hero

After improving the post-upload experience, we focused on the homepage entry point.

The homepage hero was responsible for one primary action:

Upload your PDF.

However, previous versions had:

- too much explanatory text

- large full-screen hero sections

- competing visual noise

We ran multiple iterations and A/B tests.


![](/work/issuu-homepage-preview/11.png)


## What We Learned

The best-performing version had:

- Less text

- Reduced visual noise

- A smaller hero that didn’t dominate the entire fold

- A large, centralized upload CTA

The clarity improved the perceived simplicity.

Users didn’t need marketing copy — they needed confidence.

The last header managed in the end to have a +2.3% more PDF uploads
